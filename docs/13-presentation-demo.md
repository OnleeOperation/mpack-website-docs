# 13 — Presentation Demo Guide

The website should be presented as the tangible implementation layer for the funding strategy.

# Strategic framing

Do not say:
> "We made a website because M-Pack needs a better website."

Say:
> "We created a digital hub that operationalizes the funding strategy. It gives sponsors, monthly donors, craft buyers, and community members one place to understand M-Pack and take the next step."

# 60–90 second demo path

## 1. Homepage — 15 seconds
Show:
- mission,
- what M-Pack does,
- support paths.

Suggested line:
> "The first job is clarity. A new visitor can understand M-Pack quickly and see concrete ways to engage."

## 2. Donors' Club — 15 seconds
Show:
- recurring giving explanation,
- tier/monthly support interface,
- impact updates.

Suggested line:
> "The Donors' Club creates the recurring relationship our funding strategy depends on."

## 3. Craft shop — 20 seconds
Show:
- crafts,
- activity-level story,
- purchase CTA.

Suggested line:
> "The Craft Initiative turns existing activities into a tangible supporter touchpoint. The product stays connected to the mission because the activity behind it is part of the story."

## 4. Buyer-to-supporter path — 15 seconds
Show:
- newsletter CTA or post-purchase prompt,
- Donors' Club CTA.

Suggested line:
> "The value of the craft sale does not end at checkout. The site gives that buyer a next step: follow M-Pack, subscribe, or become a recurring donor."

## 5. Corporate path — 10 seconds
Show:
- partners page,
- inquiry CTA.

Suggested line:
> "The same hub gives prospective corporate partners a clear route to learn about sponsorship and start a conversation."

# Closing line

> "Instead of three isolated fundraising ideas, the website gives M-Pack one system that can move people from awareness to participation to recurring support."

# Do not demo

- code,
- admin tools,
- unfinished forms,
- fake payment screens,
- raw internal docs,
- unverified statistics.
